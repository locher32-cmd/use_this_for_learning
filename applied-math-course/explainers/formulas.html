Formulas in plain English

The formulas you will meet most often in an applied math degree. Each one is said in everyday words, every letter is explained, and there is a small example with real numbers you can check on a calculator.

How to read any formula

  1. Letters are empty boxes for numbers. A formula is a recipe. Put your numbers in the boxes and follow the steps.
  2. Things written side by side are multiplied. $2\pi r$ means $2 \times \pi \times r$.
  3. Work in this order: brackets first, then powers, then multiply and divide, then add and subtract. A fraction bar acts like brackets around the top and around the bottom.
  4. Small numbers below a letter are just labels. $x_1$ means "the first $x$", not "$x$ times 1".
  5. Say it out loud, then test it with small numbers. If you can explain it in a sentence and get the example right, you understand it.
